# Research philosophy

> Strata doesn't exist to make completed science look impressive. It exists to make evidence inspectable, uncertainty visible, and the next unanswered question easier to investigate.

This is a product requirement. An instrument should make it possible to inspect the source, its coverage, the applicable method, and the limitations behind a displayed result. It should explain what evidence exists and what would make competing explanations easier to distinguish.

## What an instrument should help establish

- Which measurements, records, or studies were located and what they support.
- Which expected observations or records are missing, and whether the evidence establishes that they were never collected, were excluded, are inaccessible, or have yet to be discovered. Keep the cause unknown when the available records cannot make that distinction.
- Which conclusions are justified by the methods and coverage, and which require additional evidence.
- Which records, observations, or experiments could resolve an uncertainty.
- Whether a finding warrants replication because conditions, methods, or sampling coverage have changed since the original study.

Research gaps are opportunities for investigation. Environmental harm and its causes require affirmative evidence. **Never transform an absence of evidence into evidence of a particular cause.**

Strata’s founding inspiration includes the developer’s experiences with incomplete and inconsistent research involving *Crassostrea* and *Derbesia*. Case studies will follow when their records, methods, and provenance can support an evidence-based account.

## A pattern for explaining findings

1. State what the available observations establish.
2. Identify what is missing, contradictory, or inadequately measured.
3. Describe which explanations remain plausible and what each would require.
4. Identify the next observation, source record, or experiment that could distinguish them.

The first implementation applies these questions to hourly water temperature at one NOAA station. It distinguishes a missing accepted measurement from an excluded raw row, unresolved quality metadata, and a response that cannot establish coverage.
